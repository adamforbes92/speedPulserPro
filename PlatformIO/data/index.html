Open-collector hall sensors need the 12 V pull-up; push-pull sources such as Can2Cluster or a sensor with its own resistor work with None.

What Test Board does

It stops the motor for a few seconds, steps the motor rail to 5, 7.5 and 10 V and reads each one back. A pass proves the voltage injection and the rail sense, and stores this board's own voltage line. Run it once on a new board, on a 12 V+ supply. The steps are on the Diagnostics page.

More

Pulses per km = pulses per mile ÷ 1.609. The needle stops at Maximum speed or at the top calibration mark, whichever is lower. CAN, GPS and DSG drop to 0 if they stop updating. Reverse only changes with the motor stopped.

3 · Tacho (RPM)

The RPM input pin is passed to the tacho 1:1 in Hz. CAN RPM (and Test RPM) is scaled so full scale RPM gives full scale Hz.

4 · Calibrate (Drive Calibration)

  2. Raise the Drive until the needle is on your top mark, wait for Steady, enter it and Capture Mark. A low one (e.g. 40) too.
  3. Auto Fill measures the rest (1-2 minutes).

7 · Needle Sweep

Car stopped only. The speed needle sweeps the calibrated range of the dial.

Feedback

Nudges the drive to hold each mark's motor speed against load and temperature, within the trim limit and never past the top mark. If the motor feedback is lost it switches itself off. Defaults: gain 2.0, limit ±40%, deadband 0.3 Hz, off below 8.

Motor Voltage

Changed in Calibration Mode only. Afterwards, run Auto Fill again.

More

Drive = rail × PWM. Low down the rail stays at Minimum rail and the PWM falls; above Minimum rail × Nominal PWM the rail rises, up to Maximum rail (the top of the drive). Raise Maximum rail if the needle can't reach the top of a fast dial. The motor needs about 4.8 V to run smoothly at low speed.

What the causes mean

Power-on is a normal switch-on, or the supply dropping out completely (e.g. a feed that is off while cranking). Brown-out: the supply dipped low enough to reset it. A Short run is a start that reset again within 10 s: several in a row mean the supply drops out or bounces while the car starts.
